Yellow Aster Butte / Tomyhoi Peak

September 2026 | 1 day | 13.5 miles | +5600' | 

Caltopo Link

After driving back from Pemberton the night before, I hit the trail around 7am to a bluebird forecast for the next 3 days. After the week I had I was jonesing to fucking rip some trails without mercy. WA was finally ready to deliver.

Believe it or not this was my first time seeing Baker up close, cloud free.

I cut right to climb Yellow Aster. Below is Larrabee, American Border Peak and Canadian Border Peak, with Yellow Aster in the foreground. Yes, this area is very close to the CA border.

First glimpse of Tomyhoi, an odd looking peak from a distance.

"Summit" of Yellow Aster.

Insanely beautiful spot, and fairly easily accessible, and not too crowded.

Descending into the basin en route to snatch Tomyhoi. Lots of campsites here.

Sloggin up Tomyhoi approach.

Hard to even tell what the summit is from below.

This was a very obvious decision point. From trip reports I simply knew this was a Class 3 - light Class 4 climb with a sharp drop on the approach. I also had a picture of the final beta. Even armed with this information I was pretty lost as to how to get below the summit block. Going the obvious route forward on the ridge led to a sheer 300ft drop that required a rappel, which was confusing because I knew there was supposed to be a drop of some kind.

I met a pair of climbers at this point who were also having trouble figuring out how to proceed. We ended up collaborating on the effort, we had learned of a "bootpath in the snow" beta from a guy I had passed earlier. After some searching, we found a snow patch maybe 150 ft below us to the east. It definitely was not obvious to go this way, and there were several other bootpaths going in different directions into Class 4 and 5 territory, so this decision point is pretty crucial.

After dropping down, crossing two snow fields (would have been more comfortable with an axe), we climbed up some solid Class 2 wall with some tricky sections and regained the ridge, with some simple hiking to follow.

Jeff and I both agreed this was too advanced to tackle alone and very naturally starting solving it together. Turns out we were about the same skill level, and the stoke was real. He was a great climber and an absolute mad lad for climbing this thing in sandals.

We finally arrived at the drop noted in the trip reports, climbed down and prepared for the final summit block.

This was deep Class 3 with maybe one Class 4 move. Holds and rock were good, moves were easy, but the exposure was real. The route was pretty obvious through a series of slanted gullies. After the Class 4 crux the summit was a walk up.

Descending the crux was very serious work.

As the day progressed the fall colors started to pop.

A real, much needed good day.
