Broken Down

Following Panorama Ridge, I planned to drive 7.5 hours to to the Canadian Rockies, to Glacier National Park near Alberta to climb Mount Afton. I picked up some bear spray from a hardware store in Whistler, and headed northeast. About 20 minutes outside of Whistler, going full speed down highway 99, I heard a sudden metallic grinding noise from the front of my van. I came to a stop here immediately to investigate.

I assumed it was a brake failure since I had done a lot of downhill that day, but the brakes worked fine, and the engine was fine. There was a screeching metal on metal sound as I moved slowly forward and backward, coming from the wheels.

It was about 7pm on Wednesday, and I thankfully had an internet connection. I found a couple of shops to call in the morning and went to sleep.

The next morning I arranged for a shop in Pemberton to look at it the same day, and a tow to the shop, about 10 minutes north of where I broke down.

At the end of the day I learned that the right front wheel bearing had failed, a new one could be delivered the next day, and the repair could be completed end of day Thursday. You can still drive on a bad wheel bearing, so I was able to take the van a short distance to spend the night near a beautiful river, no hotels needed.

Big shout out to the little town of Pemberton, JT Heavy and Auto, and The Bread Warehouse coffee shop for locking it in for me.

At this point I decided to discontinue the Canadian Rockies trip. Being now $1400 in the hole for the tow and repairs, I couldn't justify spending the money on gas to get out there for a shortened itenerary. The weather for the North Cascades had cleared up and I decided to execute my original itenerary the last few days of my trip.
