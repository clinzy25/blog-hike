Enchantments Slam Part I

October 2026 | 1 day | 22.8 miles | +8008' | 

Caltopo Link

Inspired by the absolutely heinous Enchantments Enchainment route, I decided to piece together a similar "peak bagging" style route within my capabilities. I aimed to climb 4 of the Bulger peaks in the core zone - Little Annapurna, McClellan, Cannon, and Enchantment Peak. With no permits I would need to do this in a day, including the 5 mile trek up to Colchuck and Aasgard Pass. I was about 50% sure I would be able to accomplish all of this physically, and even less sure that I would have enough daylight, but there was no harm in trying.

If successful this would be my biggest day in the mountains by a big margin, so I decided to try some new strategies. I often have trouble eating and drinking enough in the mountains, the former because I am simply not hungry or thinking about food, and the latter because my filter water bottle restricts the flow of water, so it takes time and effort to actually drink a good amount. On this trip I packed two entire Jimmy Johns sandwiches, and utilized my fast pack's harness water bottles to have easy access to fast flowing water. I also tend to hike without stopping so I was going to force timed breaks. This may seem obvious to some but I'm still figuring out how to nail these huge days.

In typical Enchantments fashion, the day began by headlamp just before 5am with a swift ascent up to Colchuck Lake.

A boulder field and 2200' up Aasgard Pass for breakfast. My second time and no less brutal.

Topped Aasgard at about 8:30am

I stopped at a small lake to eat a sandwich and filter water. I intended for this to be the only big rest of the day, and timed it to about 50 minutes. The sandwich was glorious, and the water filtering took forever. Need to think of a better way to do this.

Looking up at Little Annapurna, the next objective.

This was a very simple task, mostly a walk up.

The summit plateau.

The views were absolutely insane. There was a sheer 3-4000 foot drop to the north, views of Stuart and the entire Enchantments basin.

Mt. Stuart, the highest non-volcanic peak in WA.

Massive views on the way down.

Back on the ground, I started traversing over to McClellan. I was feeling good, making good time, and felt fairly confident in the objective.

McClellan from below.

Above Crystal Lake and looking down the gully.

Working up the long side-hilling boulder field approach to McClellan

Gully ascent to gain the ledge in between "The Prong" and the summit, which has not been visible until about this point. The left side of this image is easier to ascend than the gully itself.

Gaining the summit ridge. I had to circumnavigate this massive boulder on the right, which required some bushwacking and giving back about 150 ft of elevation. It was more time consuming than I expected.

The summit block was a short Class 2 - 3 climb with multiple routes. I opted for a Class 2 wedge between 2 giant vertical slabs.

I was coming to terms now that I might not make my objective of all 4 peaks. Ascending McClellan took almost an hour longer than I expected, and the descent even more. I ended up downclimbing the summit block on a different route than I ascended, and got stuck on a ledge with essentially one descent option that involved a crack, one tiny foothold and a huge maneuver to drop down to a safe spot. Definitely need to be more conscious of descent routes in the future.

I targeted one of my favorite spots in the Core zone for a break - Sprite Lake.

At this point it was about 2pm, and with only about 4.5 hours of daylight left, and a long hike out, I officially decided to bail on the last 2 peaks. Physically I was pretty worked but felt like I might have been able to tackle them, but that was about to change quickly.

Ascending a roughly 500ft gully near Crystal Lake I realized my physical condition was declining sharply. I could suddenly barely manage more than a few upward steps at a time. I think it was mostly a cardiovascular issue, as I was approaching 12 hours of sustained work.

Aasgard descent was brutal as usual.

I was hitting some kind of limit on the hike out from Colchuck. Cursing the foot traffic erosion that exposed all the roots and rock on the trail, making every foot placement angled, and every step down a blow to the ankles and knees. I was 100% correct to not attempt any other peaks on this day.

Darkness fell on the forest and I found that my headlamp's plastic casing had broken and I couldn't wear it on my head, so I was carrying it in my hand, then about 1 mile from the end of the trail, it died, so I finished the hike by phone flashlight at about 8:30pm, ending a 15 hour, 8000ft, 23 mile effort.

My glorious golden chariot of a camper van awaited me. I blasted my head with cold water from my portable shower, sending me into some kind of hyperventilating state, which my cardio system did not appreciate. I had to sit in my van with my head in my hands for about 5 minutes to simply turn my body off. I decided I was too tired to drive out, ate 2 cans of chili (64 grams of protein), and slept in the parking lot.

There is nothing quite like the Enchantments. The extreme physical toll you have to pay in contrast with the rugged and pristine beauty of the region is a recipe for one of the most gratifying and rewarding mountain experiences I can think of. I am happy to have to come back at least 2 more times to bag all of the peaks in the region.

Until next time.
