Alpine Lakes High Route

August 2025 | 3 days | 35 miles | +11024' | 

Caltopo Link

First major trip of the season was heavily delayed due to a busy July and wildfires, which amazingly snuffed out all 3 of the major trips I had planned this year. This one was lower on my list and decided on it last minute. I did almost no research on the route, which complicated things. A few days after I returned from this trip, the entire region closed due to fires, likely until October.

This trip also included an attempt of Mount Hinman and summit of La Bohn Peak.

This is most ultralight I have ever packed, and I still had too much. Next trip of this length will likely be stoveless and I have been looking into bivy sacks. Finally got my toiletries whittled down to to only wipes, ibuphrophen, a bidet water bottle cap, and half a toothbrush. Though I still insist on camp shoes so I guess that makes me a poser.

I started up the Necklace Valley after a 2 hour drive from Seattle, around 7:30am. The day was mostly uneventful, passing 9 different alpine lakes, climbing about 3500ft.

I passed a group that had turned back after seeing SIX bears at this shelter. I can neither confirm nor deny this, but I did see one bear, about 100 feet from me. Unfortunately did not photograph because I was avoiding said bear.

View of La Bohn Gap, task for day 2.

I camped at the south end of the valley below La Bohn Gap.

Day 2

Up the gap 1000ft first thing. I scrambled the gully, though you can take an easier route on the left and a third route that is an offical trail that seems to not crest at the gap. This confused me and I ignored it.

View from top of gap.

Lower La Bohn Lake where I took a dip.

I immediately begian the ascent to Mount Hinman, roughly following an established trail to the east up several levels of boulder field.

I was very quickly in the alpine.

Hinman has a very long approach almost entirely boulders and some glaciers. Below is an unnamed peak on the way to Hinman.

Hinman finally comes into view, with its long summit ridge.

Mt Rainier.

Rounding the north side of the summit ridge, my route became wedge between the top of the Hinman glacier and the summit blocks. I was very difficult to traverse here, and I became aware that I was probably supposed to continue on the glacier, but I did not bring an axe and crampons to do this safely as it was quite steep.

I did find a bootpath to what I believed was the summit and completed a Class 3 scramble to the summit ridge.

But was unable to summit.

The beta for my gearless ass was definitely to continue in the glacier wedge all the way to the end of the summit ridge, and then ascend from the east. I was just kinda sketched out and worked from the super long approach. I would highly recommend just bringing an axe and traction and just walking on the glacier. Seems to be the way.

Heading back down.

The next task was ascending La Bohn Peak, which turned out to be super easy and fun.

Day 3

I ran into two major problems on day 3. First, my power bank ran out during the night and my phone was almost dead, which was a shame because I really needed it to navigate the mostly trailless high sections. Second, I underestimated the hell out of this section. I planned on hiking 14 miles out but didn't really look at the relief closely, just saw that it was mostly down. It was actually in fact mostly not down.

I first ascended from the Necklace Valley to Tank Lakes, probably the most picturesque part of the whole trip. Really wish I camped there.

The last part of the trip is censored due to swearing profusely and because my phone was almost dead.

The sections from Tank Lakes all the way out to Copper Lake were probably some of the most difficult and time consuming traversing I have dealt with. I think it took me 4 hours to go 5 miles at one point. The obstacles included long boulder fields, climbing up and down steep dirt hills with lots of brush, and sidehilling, all coupled with pretty advanced routefinding that was mentally draining. I would say maybe 10% of the entire above section was well established, normal trail. Some of the cruxy parts were unassuming but very dangerous and easy to walk into with your guard down.

At Copper Lake the route finding ceases but the trail is still pretty bad all the way to the end, extrememly steep, rocky, and with lots of blowdowns. I was feeling pretty wrecked by the end of it. Good time though, would probably let it fuck me up again.
